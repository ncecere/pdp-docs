---
title: Using agents
description: What a team member does with agents in Pi Durable Platform's console, and where people normally talk to agents instead.
---

Most people never open Pi Durable Platform's console. They talk to agents in their team's own app, such as a chat app, and the app reaches the agent through the platform's API. The console is where a team's members **test** agents, look at their conversations and manage how apps reach them.

As a member of a team you can:

- see the team's agents in **Agents**, and every agent of every team you're on in **All agents**;
- chat with an agent on its **Test** tab, in shared or private threads ([Testing an agent](/docs/use/test-chat), [Shared and private threads](/docs/use/shared-and-private-threads));
- deploy and use agents of your own in your **personal workspace**, if your platform has them ([Your personal workspace](/docs/use/personal-workspace));
- move an agent, with its conversations, between your personal workspace and teams where you're an owner or admin ([Moving an agent](/docs/use/moving-an-agent));
- create **API keys** for your own scripts ([API keys](/docs/use/api-keys));
- connect **apps** that act as you, and disconnect them ([Connected apps](/docs/use/connected-apps)).

## What you can't see

Some agents in a team are **service-managed**: an app created them for its own users, one per user. Only the team's service accounts talk to them, so you don't see them as a member. Team owners and admins see that they exist (with a **Service-managed** badge) and can delete them, but can't read them. See [Deploying agents](/docs/teams/deploy-agents#service-managed-agents).

Another person's **private** threads are invisible to you, as if they didn't exist.

## Agents that are asleep

An idle agent **hibernates**: its runtime stops, its conversations stay. The next message wakes it, which takes a few seconds. The agent's **Overview** says whether it is running now. You don't need to do anything to wake it.
