Pi Durable Platform signs people in with any OpenID Connect provider. People never get passwords from the platform itself.

## Register the client

In your identity provider, create a confidential web client:

- **Redirect URI**: `<APP_URL>/auth/callback`, for example `https://agents.example.com/auth/callback`.
- **Scopes**: `openid`, `profile` and `email` (the default `OIDC_SCOPES`), plus whatever makes your provider send groups. To [re-check groups](#re-checking-groups) while people are signed in, also `offline_access`, and allow that scope for this client.

Then set:

```sh
OIDC_ISSUER=https://idp.example.com
OIDC_CLIENT_ID=pi-durable-platform
OIDC_CLIENT_SECRET=...            # or OIDC_CLIENT_SECRET_FILE
```

The platform uses the authorization code flow with PKCE and a nonce, and verifies the ID token.

## Email addresses

Every person needs an email address, from the claim `OIDC_EMAIL_CLAIM` (`email` by default). It's how people are added to teams.

- **Verified email required.** With `OIDC_REQUIRE_VERIFIED_EMAIL=true` (the default), sign-in is refused unless the ID token has `email_verified` set to true (the boolean or the string `"true"`). A provider that doesn't send `email_verified` refuses everyone until you either make it send the claim or set the variable to `false`. Turn it off only if your provider verifies every address itself, since adding people to teams trusts the address.
- **Email domains.** `OIDC_ALLOWED_EMAIL_DOMAINS=example.com` refuses other domains.

## Groups

The platform stores each person's groups from the claim `OIDC_GROUPS_CLAIM` (`groups` by default; a list of strings or one string). Admins see them on the person's page.

`OIDC_ALLOWED_GROUPS` limits who may sign in:

```sh
OIDC_ALLOWED_GROUPS=agents-users,agents-admins
```

Only people in at least one of these groups may sign in; others see "Your account is not in a group allowed to use this platform". The bootstrap admin may always sign in, so the platform can't be locked out. It needs `OIDC_GROUPS_CLAIM`. Groups don't map to roles: platform roles are set in **Admin › Users**, team roles by each team.

Group membership is also checked **again while people are signed in**: see the next section. Email domain and verified email are checked at sign-in only.

## Re-checking groups

With `OIDC_ALLOWED_GROUPS`, the platform keeps asking your identity provider whether each person is still in an allowed group. Removing someone from the group then ends their access within a check interval, not at their next sign-in. The platform has no credentials of its own at your provider, and uses no SCIM: it uses the **refresh token** the provider gave the person at sign-in.

### What your provider must provide

1. **A refresh token for this client.** The platform asks for the `offline_access` scope, and **your provider must issue refresh tokens for it to this client**:

   ```sh
   OIDC_SCOPES=openid,profile,email,offline_access
   ```

   Some providers need the scope enabled as well: for example, a scope mapping for `offline_access` attached to the provider that serves this client. Check your provider's documentation. Where you set `OIDC_SCOPES` yourself (the platform on its own, or Kustomize: the production example sets it), add `offline_access` there. **The Helm chart adds it for you** when `oidc.allowedGroups` is set, unless you turn the re-check off with `oidc.groupRecheckInterval: "0"` (a string).
2. **The groups claim** (`OIDC_GROUPS_CLAIM`) in the ID token that a refresh returns, or else in the userinfo answer.

With `OIDC_ALLOWED_GROUPS` set but no `offline_access`, the platform logs a warning at start: without a refresh token, groups apply at sign-in only.

### How it works

At sign-in the platform keeps the person's refresh token, sealed with `ENCRYPTION_KEY`. Every `OIDC_GROUP_RECHECK_INTERVAL` (15 minutes by default) it exchanges the token at your provider's token endpoint for fresh claims, takes the groups from them, and applies the same rule as at sign-in. If your provider rotates the refresh token, the new one replaces the kept one. It checks people who were recently active: a sign-in, a live session, or an API key or connected app used in the last 30 days. A removal takes effect within the interval plus up to a minute. Set `OIDC_GROUP_RECHECK_INTERVAL=0` to turn the re-check off (with the chart, `oidc.groupRecheckInterval: "0"`; `oidc.groupRecheckGrace` sets the grace period; both are strings).

| The provider's answer | What happens |
|---|---|
| Lists an allowed group | Access continues, and the person's stored groups are updated |
| Lists none | Access ends: **no longer in a group allowed** |
| Refuses the refresh token (the person was disabled, or the grant was revoked or expired) | Access ends: **the provider no longer vouches for them** |
| Can't be asked: it is unreachable, answers with an error, or its answer has no groups claim | Nothing changes, and the platform logs a warning. When `OIDC_GROUP_RECHECK_GRACE` (24 hours by default) has passed since the provider last vouched for the person, access ends: **the provider could not confirm their groups** |

So a provider outage costs nobody access for a day, and a person the provider stops answering for loses it after one. The grace period is at least the interval.

### When access ends

For a person whose access ends, the platform:

- ends their **browser sessions**;
- **disconnects their connected apps**, which must connect again;
- forgets the stored refresh token;
- makes their **personal API keys** stop working. They aren't revoked, and work again when access is restored.

Their data, team memberships and agents stay. A conversation stream they already had open isn't closed, but every new request is refused with `403 group_denied`.

This is separate from **Suspend…**: a suspension is an admin's decision, and only an admin changes it. Group access and suspension never overwrite each other, and a person is inactive while either applies. In **Admin › Users**, the person's **Status** shows a **Group access ended** badge, with the reason on hover, and their page says why. Their entry in the audit log reads **Ended access: no longer in an allowed group**, with the reason and how many connected apps were disconnected; see [People and teams](/docs/administer/people-and-teams#users).

### Coming back

Signing in applies the rule again. When it passes, the block is lifted (**Restored access: back in an allowed group** in the audit log) and API keys work again. Connected apps must be connected again. A person still outside the group can't sign in (`403 group_denied`) and nothing changes. Removing `OIDC_ALLOWED_GROUPS` lifts every block at the next start.

### What isn't covered

- The bootstrap admin and development personas are never re-checked.
- People who signed in before the check existed, or when your provider issued no refresh token, have nothing to check with: their groups apply when they next sign in.
- A provider that lets refresh tokens expire for good (an absolute lifetime) ends access with the "no longer vouches" reason when that happens. The person signs in again.
- The stored refresh tokens are sealed with `ENCRYPTION_KEY`. `rekey` and `doctor` cover them: after a [key rotation](/docs/operate/key-rotation#what-rekey-does-and-doesnt-do), run `rekey` before you drop the previous key, and group re-checks keep working.

To cut someone off at once, whatever the interval, **Suspend…** them in **Admin › Users**: that ends their sessions and stops their keys and apps at the next request.

## The first platform admin

Set `BOOTSTRAP_ADMIN_SUBJECT` to your `sub` claim. That identity becomes a platform admin on its first sign-in, once: a later demotion isn't undone by signing in again. You can leave it set.

## Sign-in limits

`LOGIN_ATTEMPTS_PER_MINUTE` (20) limits sign-in attempts per client address. Set `TRUSTED_PROXIES` so the platform sees real client addresses behind your ingress.

## Apps and other identity providers

Apps that act as people use the platform's own OAuth Connect tokens, never the identity provider's, so they work with any provider, including ones with opaque access tokens. People still sign in to the platform through the provider when they connect an app, so these rules apply. See [OAuth Connect](/docs/build/oauth-connect).

## Development sign-in

`DEV_AUTH=true` offers fixed personas instead of a provider, and is refused unless `APP_URL` is loopback. See [Try it locally](/docs/get-started/try-it-locally).
