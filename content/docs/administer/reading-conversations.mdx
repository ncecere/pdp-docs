---
title: Reading conversations with a reason
description: How a Pi Durable Platform platform admin reads a private conversation or a personal workspace's conversation, what is recorded, and who sees the record.
---

Platform roles in Pi Durable Platform never reach conversations by themselves. Sometimes a platform admin has to read one anyway, for example to investigate a report of abuse. The platform allows it only **with a stated reason**, and records each read where the conversation's owners see it.

## Who can

- **Platform admins**, signed in to the browser. Not with an API key or a connected app.
- **Platform auditors** can't read conversations, and can't list an agent's threads either: the list is for platform admins only, and auditors don't get the **Conversations** tab.

## The Conversations tab

Platform admins see a **Conversations** tab on an agent's page: every thread of the agent, private ones too, with its visibility, who started it, who has taken part and its last activity.

- Titles of **private** conversations, and of **every** conversation of a personal workspace's agent or of a service-managed agent (an app's agent for one of its users), shared or not, are hidden: titles are content. A hidden title reads **Title hidden** (until v0.1.1, "Private conversation"); the **Visibility** column says whether the thread is private or shared.
- The card's description says which titles are hidden and that reading one takes a reason recorded in the team's audit log, which its owners can see.

In the console, the tab is on every agent's page for platform admins. Since v0.1.1 that includes every team's agents: open **Admin › Teams** › the team or workspace › **Agents**, then the agent. Before, only personal workspaces' agents and the agents of teams the admin was a member of had a page.

## Read one

<Steps>
<Step>

On the conversation's row, choose **Read…**. The dialog asks **Read this conversation?**, or names the conversation when its title isn't hidden.

</Step>
<Step>

Give a **reason** of 10 to 500 characters, then **Read conversation**. Be specific: the owners will read it.

</Step>
<Step>

The platform records the read, then shows the conversation once. Reading wakes the agent, like any read. An agent that is turned off can't be read until it's turned on again.

</Step>
</Steps>

## What is recorded

Before the conversation is returned, the platform writes `thread.admin_read` to the **audit log of the agent's team**, shown as **Read a private conversation** (or **Read a team conversation** for a shared conversation of a shared team), with:

- who read it,
- the agent and thread (`<agentId>/<threadId>`),
- the **reason**,
- whether the thread was private, and whether the workspace is personal.

The team's owners, admins and editors see it in **Team settings › Audit log**; for a personal workspace, its owner does. It's also in the platform's audit log. Each read is a new entry: reading the same conversation again takes a new reason.

## Through the API

```sh
# Every thread of an agent, private ones too (platform admins)
GET /v1/admin/agents/{agentId}/threads

# One conversation, with a reason (platform admins, browser session)
POST /v1/admin/agents/{agentId}/threads/{threadId}/read
{"reason": "Investigating a report of abuse from the helpdesk team"}

# One document an agent exposes in a conversation, with a reason (since v0.4)
POST /v1/admin/agents/{agentId}/threads/{threadId}/documents/{kind}/read
{"reason": "…"}
```

Reading a document is recorded the same way, as `thread.admin_read` with the document's kind. See [Agent state for apps](/docs/build/agent-state). Platform roles never decide tool approvals, and agent-made schedules' messages are withheld from platform staff.

See [adminListAgentThreads](/docs/api/reference/admin/adminListAgentThreads) and [adminReadAgentThread](/docs/api/reference/admin/adminReadAgentThread).
